## Practical Rules of Thumb (From Concept Index cross-references)

These terms in the Concept Index point to Munzner's actionable guidelines:

- **Expressiveness principle** (p. 100): encode only what the data contains; line charts imply ordering that categorical data lacks.
- **Effectiveness principle** (p. 101): use the most effective channel for the most important attribute.
- **Banking to 45°** (p. 157): orient line chart slopes near 45° for optimal trend perception.
- **Discriminability** (p. 106): number of distinguishable categorical values is limited per channel.
- **Separability** (p. 106): some channel pairs interfere (integral); prefer separable pairs for independent attributes.
- **Change blindness** (p. 133): animate only when object constancy is maintained; transitions must be trackable.
- **Scalability** (p. 14): explicitly consider how a design scales with data size.
- **Information density** (p. 15): maximize meaningful content per pixel, but avoid clutter.

## Key Anti-Patterns (From Index References)

- **Rainbow colormap** — still considered harmful (Borland & Taylor, p. 240)
- **3D for 2D data** — perspective distortion misleads; 3D rarely justified for abstract data (Cockburn & McKenzie, pp. 129, 141)
- **Line charts for categorical keys** — violates expressiveness; implies false trend (Zacks & Tversky, pp. 156–157)
- **Pie charts** — limited to ~5 slices; angle channel is weakest ordered channel (p. 168–170)
- **Hairball graphs** — force-directed layouts become unreadable at scale (p. 206)
- **Overplotting / occlusion** — addressed by transparency, jittering, density plots, binning (pp. 120, 307)
