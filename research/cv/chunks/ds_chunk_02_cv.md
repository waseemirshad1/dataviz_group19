## Infographic CV / Visual Resume Ideas

### CV Idea: The Personal Flower Glyph (inspired by p.53–58, Film Flowers)
- **What it visualizes:** Skills profile — multiple skill dimensions simultaneously as one unified glyph
- **How it works:** Each skill domain (e.g., psychology/behavioral science; sales/commercial acumen; software development; data science/ML; communication; creativity) is one petal of a flower. Petal length = proficiency or experience level. Petal color = domain category. The overall flower represents the candidate as a whole. More advanced skills produce longer petals. A flower with long petals in multiple directions immediately communicates broad competence.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Three major petal groups representing the three "lives": psychology/soft skills (pink tones), commercial/sales (blue tones), software/data science (green tones). The flower shape visually captures the cross-domain identity of a person pivoting from psychology + sales into data science. A viewer sees immediately that this is not a one-dimensional candidate. The data science petal could be shown as a "growing" petal (shorter but pointing in the same direction as the software petal) to communicate trajectory, not just current state.
- **Page reference:** p.53–58

### CV Idea: Compressed Life Timeline (inspired by p.92–100, My Life in Vacations)
- **What it visualizes:** Career and education timeline, with different life phases given visual prominence proportional to their relevance to the target role
- **How it works:** One row per year of life. Each "chapter" (degree, job, side project, course) is a colored rectangle within the row. Time axis compressed: years with little career development are squeezed; years with dense activity (current study period, internship, active projects) are expanded. This ensures the recent data science pivot is visually prominent even though it is short in calendar time. Within each rectangle: a small icon or texture encodes the activity type (education = book pattern; job = office icon; project = code icon; travel/conference = pin icon). Person icons or role icons above each rectangle encode context (solo work, team, client-facing).
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology degree years get a distinctive color. The sales years get another. The software development years a third. The current data science trajectory — even if only a few months — gets expanded space because it is the most relevant phase to the reader. Blur effect (from Nadieh's vacation visual, p.97) could be used for early career phases where the relevance is uncertain or skills were less developed — an honest acknowledgment of distance from those earlier roles.
- **Page reference:** p.92–100

### CV Idea: Olympic Feathers Career Arc (inspired by p.62–76, Olympic Feathers)
- **What it visualizes:** Career achievements and skill growth over time, across multiple domains
- **How it works:** Each major skill or competency domain is one "feather" arranged radially. Within each feather, each arc segment = one year or one experience. Distance from center = time (earlier = closer to center, more recent = outer edge). Color of each arc = type of activity (education, work, project, self-study). Width of arc = intensity or impact of that experience in that domain. The reader can see which domains have been consistently developed (long feathers with dense arcs) vs. which are new (short feathers just beginning to extend outward).
- **Adaptation for our context:** Feathers: "Psychological Insight / Behavioral Science," "Sales & Negotiation," "Software Engineering," "Data Science & ML," "Communication & Presentation," "Creativity & Design." The first two feathers are well-developed (long, many arcs going back years). The software feather is medium-length. The data science feather is short but the arcs are dense and recent — it is actively growing. This visual communicates the growth trajectory powerfully: the data science feather is pointing outward with momentum. Adding a world-record marker equivalent (a special mark on the most significant achievement per feather) highlights key accomplishments without a text list.
- **Page reference:** p.62–76

### CV Idea: Skill Fractal Flows by Domain (inspired by p.78–88, Dive Fractals)
- **What it visualizes:** Skill growth and cross-domain connections, presented as aesthetic flowing lines
- **How it works:** Each domain of the person's expertise is one "flow" of fractal lines, colored in that domain's hue (matching flag/identity colors from the Dive Fractals logic). The height of each fractal zone = the level of activity or intensity during that period. Zones where multiple flows converge or run close together = periods of cross-domain integration (e.g., when sales skills reinforced communication for data presentations; when psychology knowledge informed user research for software projects). The overall visual shows a career that started as separate streams and gradually converged into one integrated data-science-ready profile.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Three primary flows: psychology (warm tones), commercial/sales (cool blue), software (green). A fourth emerging flow: data science (gold/yellow). The flows begin separately in the early career and converge visually toward the present — like tributaries flowing into one river. The fractal noise intensity within each flow = variability/breadth of activities during that period (high during university = lots of variation; focused job = smoother lines). This is primarily a decorative/emotional visual — best used as a full-page hero graphic on the CV or portfolio landing page, not as a data-accurate chart.
- **Page reference:** p.78–88
