### CV Idea: The Illuminating Orb Timeline (p. 408–410)

- **What it visualizes:** Career progression as a physical-metaphor-inspired timeline where each role or achievement starts "dimmed" (early, uncertain, emerging) and becomes fully illuminated as it matures. The y-axis encodes impact or growth; the z-axis (or x-axis if 2D) encodes time.
- **How it works:** Inspired by the "One Amongst Many" installation where orbs began dim and brightened with visitor interaction. Each career stage — psychology degree, sales role, software dev, data science transition, ML6 internship — is a node on the timeline. Nodes further in the past that were more formative are placed higher (analogous to "renown" in the y-axis encoding); nodes are sized by duration or depth of learning. Color or brightness encodes how directly relevant each role is to the current data science trajectory (fully illuminated = directly relevant; dimmer = tangential but foundational).
- **Adaptation for our context:** The "dimming" metaphor maps beautifully to a non-linear career path — the psychology background and sales experience are not irrelevant, they are foundational but less immediately visible. Framing them as "dimly lit but always present" reframes a potentially awkward career change as a deliberate accumulation of diverse skills. The ML6 internship becomes the fully-bright node that the rest of the timeline has been leading toward. A hover or scroll interaction could reveal what skill each role contributed (e.g., sales → persuasion and stakeholder communication; psychology → experimental design and human behavior; software dev → technical implementation).
- **Page reference:** pp. 408–410

### CV Idea: Bidirectional Skills Network (pp. 401–404)

- **What it visualizes:** A bipartite network (inspired by the Cardcaptor Sakura character-chapter circular layout) where one dimension is "roles/experiences" and the other is "skills." Lines connect each role to the skills it developed. The result shows which skills are multiply-sourced (appear in many roles) vs. which are unique to one context.
- **How it works:** Roles/experiences (psychology degree, sales job, software dev, data science studies, ML6 internship) are arranged as pills on the outer ring. Skills (statistical thinking, communication, coding, ML, data wrangling, visualization, experimental design, client-facing) are arranged on the inner circle. A line is drawn for every role-skill connection. Hover on a skill shows all roles that contributed to it; hover on a role shows all skills it built.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Immediately communicates to a hiring manager at ML6 that the diverse background is a strength, not a liability — skills like "communication" and "understanding human behavior" trace back to multiple roles, showing depth. The visualization also reveals that core data science skills (coding, ML, visualization) are being built through multiple overlapping pathways, not just one. This is a rare CV format that rewards curious readers and makes a memorable visual impression without being gimmicky, because the structure is genuinely informative.
- **Page reference:** pp. 401–404

### CV Idea: Growth Cascade — Staggered Reveal of Competency Development (p. 415)

- **What it visualizes:** Competency levels across skill categories, revealed with a staggered animation grouped by domain. Each domain (e.g., "Data skills," "Technical skills," "Human skills," "Domain knowledge") reveals its set of competencies together with a cascade delay — communicating that skills within a domain were developed as a coherent group, not in isolation.
- **How it works:** Inspired by the group-triggered staggered lighting mechanic in the orb installation, where interacting with one orb caused its category-mates to illuminate in sequence. A static version could show skill bars arranged in domain groups, with visual cues (shared color, slight indentation) indicating group membership. An interactive web version could trigger the cascade on hover or scroll. Each bar's length encodes current proficiency; a secondary marker (e.g., a dot or tick) encodes where the skill stood 2 years ago, showing growth.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The stagger and grouping make the CV scannable at multiple levels: a quick scan reveals domain strengths; a deeper look reveals individual skill levels and trajectories. The growth marker is particularly valuable for a career-changer — it shows not just where you are but how fast you are moving. The domain grouping also bridges the psychology-to-data-science narrative: "Human skills" domain connects both worlds, while "Technical skills" domain shows the new direction.
- **Page reference:** p. 415

### CV Idea: The Participation Board — Trait Self-Map (p. 409)

- **What it visualizes:** A visualization where the candidate maps themselves on several trait axes (e.g., curious / rigorous / communicative / creative / technical), with each axis anchored by two endpoint descriptors. The result is a spider/radar chart or a dot-on-a-scale layout that makes personality profile legible at a glance.
- **How it works:** Inspired by the summary board where visitors placed star stickers to indicate which category they identified with most. In CV format: four to six trait dimensions are shown as horizontal scales or radial axes. A single marker (dot, sticker-like icon) is placed by the candidate on each axis. Brief text annotations explain why each self-placement is there — one sentence per trait citing a specific example (e.g., "Rigorous: built automated test coverage for sales pipeline tool").
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology background gives genuine credibility to self-insight claims. The sales experience supports the "communicative" and "persuasive" dimensions. The data science transition supports "curious" and "technical growth." The ML6 internship context (applied ML, client-facing projects) benefits from seeing a candidate who is self-aware about their profile. The sticker-placement metaphor also signals that this candidate is familiar with data visualization culture — a relevant signal for ML6.
- **Page reference:** p. 409
