### CV Idea: Skills as Thematic Glyph Constellation (p. 156–160)

- **What it visualizes:** Skills — both hard (data science, programming) and soft (social intelligence, negotiation, creativity)
- **How it works:** Inspired by the custom circular book glyphs from "Magic is Everywhere." Each skill category is represented as a "constellation" mark — a central node (the skill area: e.g., "Data Science," "Communication," "Psychology") with smaller satellite dots arranged around it. Each satellite dot represents a specific sub-skill or tool (Python, ML, SQL; negotiation, empathy, storytelling). Lines connect the satellites to the center and optionally between satellites that are related. The size of the central node encodes years of experience or proficiency level. The whole arrangement for all skill areas is placed in a 2D space where proximity encodes how skills relate to each other (e.g., "Data Science" and "Programming" close together; "Psychology" and "Communication" close together, bridging to Data Science to show the pivot narrative).
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology–sales–tech–data science trajectory becomes visible as a spatial journey across the skill constellation. The psychology background sits in one region, the sales/commercial skills in another, and data science tools in a third — but the social intelligence and analytical mindset skills appear as bridges, showing why the background is an asset rather than a detour. The viewer sees that the transition is not a break but a convergence.
- **Page reference:** p. 156–160

### CV Idea: Career Timeline as Scrollytelling Dot Stream (p. 177–183)

- **What it visualizes:** Work experience, education milestones, and skill acquisition over time — the career narrative
- **How it works:** Inspired by the Hamilton scrollytelling dot stream. Each dot represents a time period (a job, a study, a project). On scroll, the layout transitions: first all dots appear as an undifferentiated stream along a time axis, then they separate into "education" vs. "work" rows, then color appears to show domains (psychology / commercial / technical / data science), then each dot expands to show a mini-profile of what was gained in that period (skills, tools, context). The scrollytelling pacing controls the narrative — the viewer is guided from "here's my history" to "here's the pattern" to "here's where I'm going."
- **Adaptation for our context:** The pivot from psychology/sales to data science becomes a visible narrative arc. The dots from psychology and sales are shown first as separate, then the analytical and social skills they developed are highlighted as threads that run through all periods. The final dot (current: data science studies + this internship application) appears as a convergence point. The layout ends with a forward-looking "what comes next" region.
- **Page reference:** p. 177–183

### CV Idea: Beeswarm of Projects/Skills Clustered by Domain (p. 193–198)

- **What it visualizes:** Projects, experiences, and skills positioned along a "domain" axis (humanities ↔ technical), with size encoding depth of engagement and color encoding domain category
- **How it works:** Inspired by the Top 2000 beeswarm along a horizontal time or domain axis. Each bubble is a project, job, or course. The horizontal axis runs from "purely human/social" (psychology degree, sales) on the left to "purely technical" (ML, coding) on the right. Bubbles are clustered by beeswarm force to prevent overlap. Size encodes duration or depth (months spent, hours invested). Color encodes domain: psychology (warm), sales/commercial (orange), tech/programming (blue), data science (purple). Annotation callouts mark key milestones (e.g., "first ML project," "top sales performer," "psychology thesis on X").
- **Adaptation for our context:** The overall distribution of bubbles should visually skew toward the technical end of the axis — but with a rich cluster of social/human skills that distinguishes this candidate from purely technical applicants. The candidate's positioning across the whole axis becomes the argument: broad background, now converging on data science. The vinyl-record trick from the Top 2000 could be adapted: the "current" position (data science studies) gets a distinctive mark (e.g., a small data icon or star) to indicate it is the destination.
- **Page reference:** p. 193–198

### CV Idea: Growth Over Time — Skill Radar with Temporal Rings (p. 193–198, p. 156–160)

- **What it visualizes:** How proficiency across multiple skills has changed across 3–4 career stages (e.g., "during psychology degree," "during sales career," "now in data science studies")
- **How it works:** A radar/spider chart with 6–8 axes representing key skill dimensions (analytical thinking, domain knowledge in data, programming, communication, negotiation, creativity, domain knowledge in psychology). Instead of a single polygon, there are 3–4 concentric polygons, each one a different career stage. The polygon for the earliest stage is small and concentrated on the humanities axes. Each subsequent stage expands the technical axes while retaining the social axes. Inspired by the beeswarm rings (concentric circles per year) and the circular glyph structure from "Magic is Everywhere." The visual argument: the polygon grows more complete over time — it doesn't abandon any axis, it fills in the ones that were previously underdeveloped.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology degree fills the "analytical thinking" and "domain knowledge in human behaviour" axes strongly. Sales fills "communication" and "negotiation." Current data science studies are shown filling "programming," "ML/data skills," and "data domain knowledge" — completing the polygon. The overall shape at the end is one that ML6 can recognize: broad, analytically grounded, technically growing, human-facing.
- **Page reference:** p. 193–198, p. 156–160

### CV Idea: Domain Metaphor — "Magic is Everywhere" adapted as "Skills are Everywhere" (p. 158–164)

- **What it visualizes:** The key insight that skills from psychology, sales, and tech are not siloed — they are interconnected, and the "magic" is that transferable skills appear everywhere
- **How it works:** Directly adapts the blurred hotspot landscape from "Magic is Everywhere." Instead of fantasy book themes (magic, blood, death), the background landscape has blurred color regions for skill clusters (e.g., warm orange region = "human understanding"; cool blue = "technical"; bright yellow = "creativity"). Individual experience items (jobs, projects, courses) are placed as circles within this landscape — each one sits in the region(s) it belongs to, and some sit at the overlap between regions (e.g., a data analysis project done in a sales context sits between "human understanding" and "technical"). The blurred landscape communicates that skills are not hard boundaries but overlapping domains.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The narrative of the CV becomes visible spatially: early career items cluster in the warm/human region, later items shift toward the technical region, but the connective items (analytical sales work, psychology research methods, data-driven projects) straddle the regions. The ML6 recruiter sees: "this person has roots across the whole landscape, not just one corner."
- **Page reference:** p. 158–164 (blurred SVG hotspot regions as semantic background landscape)
