# [agent_06] Data Sketches — pages 1-50

## Infographic CV / Visual Resume Ideas

Pages 1-50 of Data Sketches are primarily the introduction, tech overview, and first two visualization projects (LotR chord diagram, Film Flowers). There is limited direct CV template content, but several structural and conceptual ideas are directly applicable.

---

### CV Idea: Career Flower Glyph — Multi-Channel Skills Portrait (p.48-50)

- **What it visualizes:** Skills portfolio — multiple skill dimensions encoded simultaneously in a single flower-shaped glyph, giving a "fingerprint" of the person
- **How it works:** One flower represents the person. Each petal group represents a skill domain (e.g., soft skills, technical skills, domain knowledge, analytical skills, creativity). Petal length/radius = proficiency level (quantitative, continuous). Petal shape = skill type (categorical: hard skill vs. soft skill vs. transferable). Color hue = origin domain (psychology background = one hue; software/data = another; sales/commercial = another). Number of petals within each group = number of distinct skills in that category.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology→sales→software→data science trajectory is naturally multi-domain. The flower encodes this as a multi-petal glyph where the dominant petals shift from social/psychological hues toward analytical/technical hues, visually showing the pivot. A version with the "data science" petals prominently longer than others signals the current trajectory. A small legend explains the encoding. Could be paired with a timeline to show which petals "grew" over time.
- **Page reference:** p.48-50

### CV Idea: Loom-and-Strings Career Flow — Connections Between Education and Roles (p.28-43)

- **What it visualizes:** The flow from degrees/education (outer arc) to job roles/experiences (inner sections), showing how past domains connect to the present trajectory
- **How it works:** Outer ring = educational domains and skill clusters (Psychology, Computer Science/Dev, Sales/Commercial, Data Science). Inner center = career roles or time periods (e.g., Student, Sales Rep, Software Developer, Data Science Student). Strings connect outer domains to inner roles with thickness proportional to how much time/effort was spent at that intersection. Color per outer domain.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The "hacked chord diagram" origin story (Nadieh mentions turning a chord diagram into a flow chart showing how students went from educational degree to job type — p.32, sidebar) is a direct analogue. Here the same structure shows: Psychology degree → Sales roles (thick string), Psychology degree → Social Intelligence skills → all roles (thinner strings), Software Dev → Data Science (growing string). The visual argument: multiple backgrounds converge into data science, not a single linear path.
- **Page reference:** p.32 (sidebar), p.34-38

### CV Idea: "Film Flowers" Career Timeline — One Glyph per Job/Experience (p.44-50)

- **What it visualizes:** Work experience and education timeline — each role or study period rendered as a glyph (like a film flower), positioned on a timeline
- **How it works:** x-axis = time (years). Each job/study period = one glyph. Glyph size = duration of that role (larger = longer). Glyph color = domain (psychology/social, commercial/sales, technical/dev, data science). Petal shape = role type (education, employment, freelance, project). Petal count or radius = a "growth" or "impact" score (self-assessed or based on skills gained). The result is a timeline of career glyphs that grows in technical color intensity from left to right, culminating in data science.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology degree (one color), sales roles (another color), software development (technical color), current data science study (bright accent) — arranged left to right chronologically. The visual trajectory shows the pivot: early warm/social hues, then a transition to cooler technical hues. Internship application context: the rightmost glyph (data science student) is the largest and most prominent, signaling current focus.
- **Page reference:** p.44-50

### CV Idea: Manually Enriched Data Principle — Annotating the CV with Context Variables (p.31-32)

- **What it visualizes:** Any CV section, but specifically the idea of adding variables that don't "naturally" exist in a standard CV — e.g., skills gained per role, not just job titles
- **How it works:** Standard CV lists job title, company, dates. Enriched version adds manually annotated variables: "primary skill developed," "domain of knowledge," "soft vs. hard skill," "relevance to data science." These become the encoding channels in the visual (color, petal type, etc.). Like Nadieh manually adding location to the LotR dataset, the designer manually adds meaning-bearing variables to each CV entry.
- **Adaptation for our context:** For the psychology→data science pivot, manually adding a "data science relevance" score (1-5) to each past role and skill, then encoding it as color saturation or position, makes the trajectory argument visually compelling even if past roles were not technically in data science.
- **Page reference:** p.31-32
