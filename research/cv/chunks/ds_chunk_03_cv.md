### CV Idea: The Color Journey — Skills as Extracted Hues (p. 103–111)

- **What it visualizes:** Skills and competencies across domains (psychology, sales, software development, data science), where each domain has its own "color palette" and the overall CV shows the blended palette of a career.
- **How it works:** Inspired by the travel colors project: each domain or job role is a "trip," and each skill gained is a "color extracted" from that experience. The radial layout places the CV owner at the center, with career domains arranged as angular segments. Individual skills are dots positioned at an angle (within their domain segment) and at a radius corresponding to proficiency level. Color encodes the domain (psychology = warm tones, sales = green, software = blue, data science = purple/gold). The full radial "constellation" shows the career as a color field — diverse at the periphery, convergent at the center.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Psychology → warm amber/orange dots (soft skills, empathy, research methods); Sales/commercial → green dots (negotiation, stakeholder management, communication); Software development → cool blue dots (coding, system thinking, automation); Data science trajectory → bright gold/yellow dots at the growing edge of the radial. The career pivot is visible as a gradient from warm to cool to bright — the "newest" colors (data science) are the most vivid and furthest from center, suggesting frontier and growth.
- **Page reference:** p. 107–111

### CV Idea: Constellation of Competencies — Network of Skills with Opacity as Mastery (p. 119–128)

- **What it visualizes:** All skills and traits as nodes in a network, where connections encode which skills reinforce each other and opacity encodes current mastery level.
- **How it works:** Inspired by Royal Constellations: dark background, glowing nodes. Each node is a skill or trait (e.g., "empathy," "Python," "negotiation," "statistical thinking"). Edges connect skills that are mutually reinforcing (e.g., empathy connects to "user research," "communication," "UX thinking"). Nodes closest to the "data science" anchor node are fully opaque and large; more distant skills (earlier career, less currently relevant) are smaller and semi-transparent. The layout gravitationally pulls all nodes toward their closest domain anchor (like the 10 royal families → 3–4 domain anchors for this CV).
- **Adaptation for our context:** Domain anchors: Psychology / Sales / Tech / Data Science. Nodes gravitate to their strongest domain but connections cross domain lines (showing transfer of skills). The psychology → data science story is told by the position of high-opacity nodes: they have moved from the psychology quadrant to the data science quadrant, with threads still connecting back to soft skills. Mastery = opacity (high mastery = bright, low = dimmed). Ambition/learning trajectory = slightly larger glowing ring around actively developing skills.
- **Page reference:** p. 119–128

### CV Idea: Scrollytelling Career Narrative — Same Marks, Changing Lens (p. 137–143)

- **What it visualizes:** Work experience, education, and skills — all as a single set of marks that rearrange under scroll to reveal different organizational structures.
- **How it works:** Inspired by the Obama headshot grid → timeline scroll transition. Each "mark" is a small icon or glyph representing a life/career event (degree, job role, project, skill acquired). Section 1: arranged as a grid by domain (psychology / sales / software / data science). Section 2: same marks rearrange chronologically (timeline). Section 3: same marks rearrange by skill type (technical / analytical / interpersonal). Section 4: highlights the pivot moments — jobs or projects that visibly bridge between domains (connecting lines appear between the bridging events). The transition itself shows the career is coherent viewed from any angle.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The psychology degree mark links to sales jobs (emotional intelligence, persuasion) and also to data science (research methodology, hypothesis testing). The software development marks link to data science. The pivots are highlighted with a connecting arc when section 4 is reached, showing that the career is not a series of unrelated jumps but a coherent accumulation. Section 5 zooms in on the current position: studying, building toward ML/data science — with a forward-pointing arrow or projection.
- **Page reference:** p. 137–143

### CV Idea: Dual-Radius Competency Bubbles — Breadth vs. Depth (p. 141)

- **What it visualizes:** Skills, where each skill has both breadth (how widely applied across jobs/projects) and depth (how advanced the mastery).
- **How it works:** Inspired by the video emotion glyph (filled circle + outer ring). Each skill is a circle: inner filled radius = depth of mastery (larger = more expert); outer ring radius = breadth of application across different contexts (larger = applied in more domains or jobs). Skills clustered by domain (soft skills / technical / analytical), with x-position encoding time since first acquired (older skills to the left, newer to the right). A dot on the ring marks each domain where the skill has been applied. Result: at a glance, you see which skills are deep but narrow (small outer ring, large inner circle), which are broad but shallow (large outer ring, small inner circle), and which are both deep and broad (large on both).
- **Adaptation for our context:** "Negotiation" — large inner radius (high mastery from years in sales) and large outer ring (applied in sales, team projects, freelance, academic contexts). "Machine learning" — small inner radius (still developing) but growing outer ring as it is applied in more contexts. "Empathy/social intelligence" — distinctive shape: very large outer ring (applied everywhere from psychology to sales to team collaboration), moderate inner radius. The unusual shapes become memorable visual identifiers.
- **Page reference:** p. 141

### CV Idea: Fisheye Focus — Expandable Skill Detail on Hover (p. 143)

- **What it visualizes:** A dense skill inventory where each skill can be expanded for detail without losing the overall landscape.
- **How it works:** Inspired by the fisheye timeline. The CV presents all skills as a dense horizontal band — each skill is a small labeled bar or bubble. The fisheye distortion magnifies the region under the cursor, expanding hovered skills to show detail (years of experience, specific tools/contexts, related projects) while compressing adjacent skills. The full landscape remains visible. This is particularly useful in a digital/interactive CV format.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The horizontal axis groups skills by domain (left = psychology/soft skills, right = data science/technical). The career trajectory is visible as a density shift: the rightmost (data science) region is the newest and still growing, shown with slightly lower density of items but higher "energy" (brighter color, larger default size). Hovering the data science cluster reveals current coursework, tools being learned, and the ML6 internship target.
- **Page reference:** p. 143
