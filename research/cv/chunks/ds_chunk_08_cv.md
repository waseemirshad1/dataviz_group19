# [agent_13] Data Sketches — pages 351-400

## Source Context
Pages 351–400 cover three visualization projects: Nadieh's star-constellation map "Figures in the Sky", Shirley's 3D crystal glyph display "Legends", and the opening of Nadieh's radial manga visualization. The most relevant CV design inspirations come from: (1) the crystal glyph field where each item is a multi-faceted, multi-variable gem representing an individual entity; (2) the ring-of-thumbnails overview + detail structure; and (3) the progressive 3D disclosure pattern (ground-level personal detail vs. bird's-eye holistic pattern).

---

### CV Idea: Crystal Glyph — Career as a Faceted Gem (p.371–377)

- **What it visualizes:** Each major role, degree, or achievement is a faceted 3D crystal (or a flat illustrated equivalent). Multiple visual channels encode: how long the role lasted (size), how many skills it added (number of facets), domain area (color family), and recency (z-position or vertical position on the page).
- **How it works:** Inspired by Shirley's Nobel Laureate crystals: size = influence/duration, face count = multi-dimensionality (how many skill domains the role touched), color = domain family (psychology / software / data science / sales). Placed in a timeline arrangement. Viewing it flat shows the roles; a hoverable detail on each crystal reveals what was learned and which skills it contributed.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Psychology degree = a wide, many-faceted gem (multi-domain, long duration); software development role = a tall narrow gem (focused skill depth); ML6 internship = the most recent, largest, and most colorful gem in the foreground. Sales background = a gem with a distinct warm color hue, showing the "commercial bridge" between psychology and tech. The gender/representation framing of the original can be adapted to a "growth framing" — each gem is a step in an intentional career pivot.
- **Page reference:** p.371–377

### CV Idea: Ring of Thumbnails — Skill Domains as a Surrounding Ring with Central Focus (p.360–361)

- **What it visualizes:** A central circle represents the target role (ML6 data scientist). Surrounding it in a ring are "thumbnail" circles, one per skill domain or experience area (statistics, psychology, sales, Python/SQL, ML, communication). Clicking/hovering a thumbnail expands it in the center with supporting evidence (courses, tools, projects).
- **How it works:** Directly adapted from the "ring of mini sky maps" pattern — each small circle shows a constellation (skill area), clicking it brings up the full version (details, evidence). The central circle always shows the current target state (the role you want), and each surrounding circle shows how you connect to it.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Works as an interactive web CV or as a static print layout. For print: use the ring layout to immediately communicate "breadth of background connecting to one target" — the visual structure makes the pivot story legible at a glance. The central target is ML6 internship; surrounding rings are: (1) psychology research methods, (2) behavioral sales experience, (3) software development / coding, (4) data analysis, (5) machine learning coursework, (6) communication/presentation.
- **Page reference:** p.360–361

### CV Idea: Multi-Ring Radial Timeline — Degrees / Roles / Skills as Concentric Layers (p.389–400)

- **What it visualizes:** A radial layout inspired by the CCS multi-ring design. Innermost ring = life timeline (years). Second ring = education arcs (colored by institution/domain). Third ring = work experience arcs. Outermost ring = skill acquisition (tools, frameworks, methods) — each skill appears as a small arc in the year it was first used. Connection lines from a work/study arc to the skills it produced.
- **How it works:** Time runs around the circle (or as an arc, e.g., 2018–2026). Each concentric ring encodes a different "layer" of the career. Connections show "this degree led to these skills" and "this job produced these tools." The multi-ring structure visually encodes growth: the outer ring fills up over time, showing increasing skill breadth.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Psychology background occupies a large arc of the education ring (several years). Software development experience fills an adjacent work arc. The pivot is visible as the outer skill rings growing outward: early = psychology methods; mid = programming languages; recent = ML tools and data science frameworks. ML6 internship appears at the very recent end — the outermost arcs fill most densely there, showing accelerated growth.
- **Page reference:** p.389–400

### CV Idea: Progressive Disclosure 3D — Walk Through Skills, Fly Above for Pattern (p.377)

- **What it visualizes:** A 3D scatter where each role/experience is a point. X = technical depth, Y = people/communication skills, Z = time (recency). At ground level the viewer sees individual roles and details. From above, the trajectory becomes a path from psychology (high communication, low technical) toward data science (high technical, maintained communication) — showing the intentional diagonal movement.
- **How it works:** Uses Three.js or a flat 2D approximation (isometric projection). The z-axis encodes time, so the most recent roles are "in the foreground." The unique insight: the CV demonstrates the candidate is someone who has grown continuously in a specific direction, not just accumulated random experience.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The "progressive disclosure" narrative maps to the job application story: first impression = recent ML6-adjacent skills and projects (ground level = what you see first); zooming out = the full career arc that makes the pivot coherent (bird's eye = the whole story). This is a persuasive framing tool as much as a CV design.
- **Page reference:** p.377

### CV Idea: Annotation-First Scatter — Skills vs. Industry Demand with Editorial Callouts (p.363, p.399)

- **What it visualizes:** A scatter plot of ~15–20 skill areas. X = industry demand at ML6 / in data science roles (based on job descriptions). Y = your current proficiency. Points in the upper-right = ready-to-deploy skills; points in the lower-right = skills being developed (growth narrative); points in the upper-left = unique differentiators (psychology background = high proficiency, sometimes undervalued by typical ML candidates).
- **How it works:** Heavy editorial annotations callout the most important quadrants. The upper-left "unique differentiator" quadrant is explicitly annotated as a value-add: "Psychology background = understanding user behavior, survey design, causal reasoning — rare in ML candidates." The lower-right "development zone" is annotated with completion dates or current coursework.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Psychology methods (experiment design, statistics, behavioral analysis) sit in the upper-left — high proficiency, potentially undervalued. Python/ML frameworks sit in the lower-right — rapidly developing. Sales + communication experience sits in the upper-left with a different annotation ("bridge to client-facing ML work"). The annotation-first approach lets you editorially direct the recruiter's eye exactly where you want it, rather than leaving them to read through a list.
- **Page reference:** p.363, p.399
