# [agent_10] Data Sketches — pages 201-250

## Source context
Pages 201–250 cover three project narratives (DDR spirals, Dragon Ball Z fight network, Harry Potter fanfiction). These are process narratives rather than CV design theory, so direct CV design inspiration is sparse. However, several structural and encoding ideas from these pages translate meaningfully to an infographic CV context.

---

### CV Idea: Spiral Career Timeline (inspired by p.209–211)

- **What it visualizes:** Career / education timeline — the full trajectory from psychology degree through sales/commercial work to software development and now data science studies.
- **How it works:** A single Archimedean spiral where the arc length represents time (e.g., 1 year of arc per revolution). Key career events are placed as dots along the spiral at the correct temporal position. Dot color encodes type of event (education = one color, job = another, skills milestone = another). Dot size encodes significance/duration. The spiral grows outward over time, metaphorically representing expanding capability. The center is the starting point (psychology degree); the outer edge is the present.
- **Adaptation for our context:** The spiral's continuous, non-breaking form is semantically meaningful: the career has no gaps — each phase flowed into the next. The pivot from psychology → sales → software → data science is visible as a smooth outward arc rather than a list of disconnected items. The spiral can be annotated at each revolution with a year label. A small cluster of tightly spaced dots at the outer edge represents the current acceleration phase (multiple skills acquired recently).
- **Page reference:** p.209–211

### CV Idea: Difficulty-as-Size Encoding for Skill Proficiency (inspired by p.211)

- **What it visualizes:** Skill proficiency — how advanced the person is across different skill areas.
- **How it works:** In the DDR visualization, difficulty was encoded inversely as circle size — harder songs had smaller circles (a pun: harder to see). For a CV, invert this back to the natural direction: circles or bars where size encodes proficiency level directly. Arrange skills in a radial or grid layout, sized by proficiency. Color-code by skill domain (soft skills = one hue, technical/data science = another, languages/tools = a third). This avoids the clichéd "progress bar" while still encoding a quantitative proficiency signal.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Three domains map well to three color families: psychology/soft skills (warm), commercial/sales (neutral), data science/technical (cool blue). The visual would show the career pivot story through color composition: a mix of warm and neutral in the past, increasingly cool/technical toward the present.
- **Page reference:** p.211

### CV Idea: Swoosh Alignment — From Social to Technical (inspired by p.226)

- **What it visualizes:** The career pivot narrative — showing movement from one domain (social/commercial) to another (analytical/technical).
- **How it works:** In the DBZ visualization, characters migrated across the good/bad divide spatially — their lines literally moved from right to left as they changed allegiance. For a CV: draw the career as a vertical timeline with two "sides" (left = social/commercial orientation, right = analytical/technical orientation). Each job or study milestone is a node. Connecting lines between nodes swoosh toward the technical side over time, visually showing the pivot. The swoosh direction across the timeline makes the career direction change legible at a glance without a single word.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Node at top = psychology degree (left-leaning swoosh). Node = sales jobs (center to left). Node = first software development work (moving right). Node = current data science study (fully right). The visual story is the migration of swoosh direction — an elegant metaphor for a career pivot.
- **Page reference:** p.226

### CV Idea: Network Graph of Skills as Navigation (inspired by p.241)

- **What it visualizes:** Skill interconnections and the integrative nature of the psychology + sales + software → data science combination.
- **How it works:** A node-link graph where each node is a skill or competency domain. Edges connect skills that reinforce each other (e.g., "negotiation" connects to "stakeholder communication" and "experiment design"; "psychology" connects to "behavioral analytics" and "user research"). The layout clusters related skills. Color encodes skill domain origin. Node size encodes demonstrated proficiency or recency. Crucially, the network shows that the psychology and sales background is not disconnected from data science — it is structurally connected through shared nodes (analytical thinking, human behavior modeling).
- **Adaptation for our context:** The network makes the non-obvious argument: the psychology + commercial background is a feature, not a liability — it creates a richer connected graph than a purely technical background would. The density and cross-domain connections tell a story of breadth. A sidebar panel (inspired by the click-to-navigate pattern) could show examples or evidence for each skill node when clicked.
- **Page reference:** p.241

### CV Idea: Dot Histogram of Output over Time (inspired by p.236–237)

- **What it visualizes:** Productivity and output over time — projects completed, skills acquired, roles held — analogous to the fanfiction publication timeline.
- **How it works:** A horizontal timeline where each dot represents one notable output (a project, a certification, a job milestone, a course completed). Dots are stacked vertically per time period (e.g., per 6-month block). Dot color encodes type of output (education / work project / personal project / tool/skill acquired). The overall shape of the histogram shows acceleration over time — a key signal for a career-pivoting candidate: recent periods should be taller (more outputs), demonstrating momentum.
- **Adaptation for our context:** Would visually demonstrate that the current study period is highly productive compared to earlier phases. Color gradient within dots could encode relevance to AI/data science (dark = highly relevant, light = background). Annotate key spikes (e.g., "started data science bootcamp," "first ML project").
- **Page reference:** p.236–237
